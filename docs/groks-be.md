1\. The Two-System Brain Architecture (The Foundation)

Daniel Kahneman’s Nobel-winning framework from Thinking, Fast and Slow is the bedrock:



System 1: Fast, automatic, emotional, effortless. Runs on heuristics (mental shortcuts). Feels like “intuition.”

System 2: Slow, deliberate, logical, effortful. Requires attention and working memory.



Most decisions — especially consumer ones — are System 1. Marketing/scams are engineered to hijack System 1 and keep System 2 offline. Stress, cognitive load, time pressure, or emotional arousal all weaken System 2 further. Result: people “know” it’s dumb but click anyway.

3\. Why This Is So Predictable and Common (But Feels Alien to You)



~95% of daily decisions are System 1 (automatic).

You personally may score higher on conscientiousness, need for cognition, or skepticism — traits that strengthen System 2 engagement. That makes the behavior feel “alien” to you while it’s the default for the majority.

Hey woflfren, solid foundational questions — love that you're digging into the roots of this. I'll hit 'em straight, backed by the established field (no fluff, just replicated core stuff plus fresh 2026 context from trends).

1\. Best Overall Term

The most widely accepted and commonly used descriptor is Behavioral Economics.



It's the interdisciplinary field (psychology + economics) that systematically studies why people make irrational decisions, cognitive biases, heuristics, and predictable deviations from "rational" models.

Pioneered by Kahneman \& Tversky (heuristics/biases), expanded by Thaler (Nudge, Nobel 2017).

Closely related/broader terms: Behavioral Science (umbrella including psych, sociology) or Judgment and Decision Making (JDM) in pure psych depts.

For the marketing angle you mentioned: Consumer Psychology or Behavioral Marketing.



But if you want the one term pros use most? Behavioral Economics — it's got the journals, Nobels, conferences (e.g., ongoing SABE 2026 events), and real-world impact.

Personalized wellness \& mental health apps: Using nudges for habit formation, but ethically (e.g., AI coaching for sleep/exercise/finance). Behavioral health is booming — trends show operational redesign + value-based care needing behavioral tools, yet many health plans underuse them.

Online education \& CPD (continuing professional dev): Integrating nudges into digital learning platforms to boost completion rates/motivation.

AI-human interaction: Countering AI fatigue/trust issues with behavioral design (e.g., ethical personalization without creepy overreach).



Small/one-person ops? Absolutely viable.
